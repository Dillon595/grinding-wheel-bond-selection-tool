# Grinding Wheel Bond Selection Tool

A browser-based technical guide for selecting a preliminary **diamond or CBN grinding wheel bond** based on workpiece material, grinding operation, productivity requirements, surface finish, and other process conditions.

## What Is Grinding Wheel Bond?

The bond is the matrix or construction system that holds abrasive grains in the grinding wheel.

For superabrasive grinding wheels, bond selection strongly affects:

- Abrasive retention
- Wheel life
- Cutting behavior
- Form holding
- Dressing requirements
- Surface finish
- Material removal
- Thermal behavior

## Bond Types Covered

### Resin Bond

Resin bonds are widely associated with finishing and precision grinding applications where good cutting action and surface finish are important.

Typical considerations include:

- Good cutting characteristics
- Suitable for many precision applications
- Often used with diamond and CBN
- Bond wear and thermal behavior must be considered

### Vitrified Bond

Vitrified bonds are rigid, porous structures that can provide strong form retention and effective coolant access in suitable grinding applications.

Typical considerations include:

- Strong form retention
- Porosity
- Dressing capability
- Suitable for many precision and production grinding applications

### Metal Bond

Metal bonds can provide strong abrasive retention and good wear resistance.

Typical considerations include:

- Long wheel life
- Strong abrasive retention
- Good form holding
- Dressing can require specialized methods

### Electroplated Wheels

Electroplated grinding wheels typically use a single abrasive layer attached to a wheel body.

Typical advantages include:

- High abrasive exposure
- Useful for complex profiles
- High stock removal potential
- Can be used where conventional multi-layer wheels are unsuitable

The appropriate construction depends on the specific application.

## Common Questions

### What bond should I use for a diamond grinding wheel?

The appropriate bond depends on the workpiece, grinding operation, required finish, wheel geometry, stock removal, and dressing process. Resin, metal, vitrified, and electroplated constructions each have different characteristics.

### What bond is commonly used with CBN wheels?

CBN wheels can be manufactured with resin, vitrified, metal, or other construction approaches depending on the application. The best choice depends on grinding requirements rather than abrasive type alone.

### What is the difference between resin and vitrified bond?

Resin and vitrified bonds differ in structure, stiffness, porosity, dressing behavior, thermal characteristics, and wheel performance. The better choice depends on the grinding process.

### When should I use a metal bond grinding wheel?

Metal bond can be attractive when strong abrasive retention, wear resistance, and form holding are important.

### What is an electroplated grinding wheel?

An electroplated wheel generally contains a single working layer of abrasive attached to the wheel surface. This construction is often useful for profiles, complex geometries, and applications requiring high abrasive exposure.
